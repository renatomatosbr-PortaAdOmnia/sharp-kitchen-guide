Why it happens

Most cheap knives are made of soft, stamped steel

Many budget knives are stamped out of thin sheets of softer steel. Soft steel is easy to sharpen, but the edge rolls over quickly once it meets a cutting board. Japanese-style knives usually use harder steel and a thinner edge angle, which is why they're known for staying sharp longer and gliding through food.

How to choose

3 things a good everyday chef knife should have

  • Hard, quality steel. It's what decides how long the edge lasts between sharpenings.
  • A comfortable, balanced handle. You should be able to chop for ten minutes without your hand getting tired.
  • An all-purpose shape. One knife that slices, dices and chops covers almost everything you cook.

3 habits that keep any knife sharp longer

  • Use a wood or plastic board. Glass, stone and ceramic boards dull an edge fast.
  • Wash it by hand and dry it right away. Dishwashers knock the edge against other items.
  • Store it in a block, on a magnetic strip or in a sheath, not loose in a drawer.

Always handle sharp knives with care, cut away from your body and keep them out of reach of children. Follow the maker's care instructions.
